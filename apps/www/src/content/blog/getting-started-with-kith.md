## Start with something you would otherwise put off

You do not need a perfect prompt. Pick one small job with a result you can recognize. “Help me prepare for my meeting tomorrow” is a useful start. A little context makes it better: which meeting, what you care about, and how much detail you want.

Try this:

> Find my next meeting, review its agenda and any relevant email, and give me a short brief with three questions to ask. Link to the sources you use.

Connect Calendar and email if you want Kith to check those accounts. It can only use information you share or allow it to access. If the meeting has no agenda, the helpful result is to say so and identify what you still need.

## Turn a long thread into a clear next step

An email thread can contain a decision, a changed date, and a question no one answered. Ask Kith to separate those pieces before writing a reply.

> Summarize this thread. What did we agree on, what is still open, and what do I need to do next? Draft a short reply for me to review. Do not send it.

Check the draft against the original thread. Add the detail that only you know, then decide what to send. A draft can save time without making a commitment on your behalf.

## Compare options before you choose

Give Kith the criteria you would use yourself: budget, timing, location, or a feature you cannot compromise on.

> Compare three project-management tools for a team of five. We need shared tasks and simple reminders. Keep the total under $50 a month, check current prices, and link to each source. Do not sign up for anything.

Prices and features change. Open the links, check the date, and ask Kith to flag anything it could not confirm. If the options do not meet your requirements, refine the request rather than choosing from a poor shortlist.

## Keep the useful context

When something becomes a preference, tell Kith: “Keep meeting briefs under five bullet points.” When a detail is temporary, say that too: “This budget is only for this project.”

Saved memories help future tasks, but they are not a substitute for checking changing facts. Review them in the app and remove anything you no longer want used.

## Make the next handoff easier

A helpful request names four things: the job, the information to use, the result you want, and the decisions that should stay with you.

Start small. Once a task works well, you can ask for a scheduled routine or create a focused bot for that job. You can still return to your everyday Kith conversation for everything else.
