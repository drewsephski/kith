## Choose the work before the app

Think of something you postponed this week. Maybe you still need to prepare for a meeting, find an email, or narrow a set of options. That is a better starting point than asking which assistant has the most features.

Write down what “done” would look like. A meeting brief might need the agenda, a few questions, and links to relevant documents. An email task might need a summary and a draft you can edit. Keep the first test small enough to check yourself.

## Find the interface you want to return to

Kith gives you an ongoing personal-assistant conversation, with optional bots for focused jobs. Grok Bot also uses named bots and ongoing context. OpenClaw focuses on connecting an assistant to chat services, while Hermes provides desktop and terminal interfaces.

You can explore those differences in the [assistant comparisons](/alternatives/). Product access, plans, and available features can change, so check the linked official sources before choosing.

The right interface is the one you can find your work in again. It should be clear where results land, where questions wait, and how to return to a task.

## Test context, not just a polished answer

Give the assistant a preference: “Keep this brief under five bullets.” Then ask a follow-up. Does it use that preference without losing important information? Can you change or remove what it remembers?

An assistant that remembers an old deadline still needs to check today’s calendar. Look for a distinction between saved context and current facts. A confident answer without a source is harder to trust than a clear explanation of what was found.

## Connect accounts one task at a time

Calendar access makes sense for meeting preparation. Email access makes sense for finding a thread. Neither is needed to help rewrite a document you share.

Read the permissions before connecting. Check how to disconnect an account and where you review saved memories. Give the assistant enough access to help with the job, then see whether the result is worth it.

## Keep decisions that matter with you

Tell the assistant what it may prepare and what needs your review. “Draft a reply, but do not send it” is a useful boundary. So is “Compare options, but do not buy anything.”

Check how the app handles approval requests and stopping work. Look at the task history to understand what happened. Important dates, commitments, and payments deserve your own review.

## Make one good task a habit

When the first result is useful, give feedback: what to shorten, what to remember, and which sources matter. Try it again on another day before turning it into a routine.

In Kith, you can keep using your main conversation or add a bot for a recurring job. Start with [your first useful task](/blog/getting-started-with-kith/) and build from there.
